Zero-cohort RMSE falls from 3.31 to 0.25. That part is cohort detection: those 37 files are louder and less silent than every other train file, and the model predicts them near 0 (mean prediction 0.088, max 1.01) without seeing the filename.

The rest of the gain is smaller but real. On the other 732 rows, RMSE falls from 1.028 (E001) to 0.782 (E004), and MAE from 0.879 to 0.633. Acoustic features are not only a zero detector.

E004 still shrinks. On those 732 rows, prediction std is 0.60 against target std 1.01. Low scores are pulled up (bin 1–2: mean prediction 3.04 vs mean target 1.98). High scores are pulled down (bin 4.5–5: mean prediction 4.08 vs mean target 4.86). The largest residuals are ordinary files, not the zero cohort. Worst misses are 5.0 predicted near 2.7–3.2, and the rare 1.0/1.5 predicted near 3.1–3.6. That is the mean-reversion expected from acoustic proxies of fluency, not grammar.
